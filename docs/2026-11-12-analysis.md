# Three questions, answered — 12 November 2026

**Status:** analysis only. Nothing in §1 or §2 of this document has been built, and the
decisions it points at are the author's. The only thing shipped on this date is the material
pass on the cover and the scroll, which touches no chapter and is reported at the end.

**What prompted it:** the author's §1.7, *human-led motion*, recorded in the design document
and in CLAUDE.md on the same date. The three questions are (1) what the mechanics become when
the reader leads, (2) whether the graphics can go up another order of magnitude or whether the
idiom is the ceiling, and (3) where the cost actually is.

# 1 · The mechanics, from first principles, under human-led motion

## 1.1 What the book does today, stated exactly, because it is the thing being replaced

Every one of the five live chapters has the same spine:

```
if (started) t += dt;
const sched = B.value(t);            // app/js/breath.js — pure f(t)
const target = reachAt !== null ? reachFill(reachAt) : schedFill;
const gap    = target - body.fill;
body.fill   += clamp(gap * PURSUE_K * dt, ±rateCap * pace * dt);
```

`PURSUE_K = 14`, `DEAD = 0.012` of the band, `pace = 1` when a finger is present and
`SELF_FLY = 0.45` / `SELF_WALK = 0.42` when it is not. The rate cap comes from the protocol:
`fillPerSec = 1/din`, `emptyPerSec = 1/dout`, holds counting toward neither
(`app/js/course.js:90-101`).

Three facts follow, and they are the whole of the problem.

1. **The reader's input is a position in a band, never a time.** Long Air, the Shepherd and
   the Murmuration all read one finger's `clientY` into 0..1 and nothing else. Pearl Diver
   reads a single `pointerdown` and ignores both its duration and its position. Bamboo Rain
   reads a press/release boolean.
2. **Nothing anywhere measures the reader's own rate.** No period tracker, no turning-point
   detector, no inter-press interval, no derivative of `reachAt`, no history of any kind.
   `reachAt` is read in seven places per chapter and every one is a present-tense read. The
   single accumulator in the codebase that touches reader timing is `heldFor` in
   `invite.js`, compared once against `minHold = 0.35 s` to decide whether a press counted as
   comprehension. It feeds the affordance and nothing else.
3. **Every error in the book is measured against an app-owned schedule value.** The pursuit
   target, Long Air's gate centres (`gate.centre = schedule(ta)`, baked at build time),
   `drag`'s `working` test (`ph.k === 'in' ? inDown && !outDown : ...`), the Shepherd's
   `targetHills`. Take the schedule away and there is nothing left to subtract from.

So the reader is already expressing a *shape* and never a *rate*, and the app has never once
looked at their timing. That is the gap §1.7 names.

## 1.2 What a rate becomes

A rate today is a schedule: an ordered list of `(kind, duration)` that a clock walks. Under
§1.7 it cannot be, because a schedule is a sequence of times and the reader owns the times.

Three things survive when the times are removed:

- **An ordering.** in → hold → out → rest. The cycle's shape, not its length.
- **A ratio.** 4-4-4-4 is "equal four ways"; 4/8 is "twice as long out as in". A ratio is
  scale-free, which is precisely why it can survive a rate change by construction.
- **A preferred period.** The rate we would like them to arrive at — now a preference, not a
  fact.

> **A rate becomes a ratio plus a preferred period, and the reader supplies the actual
> period.**

The engine's primitive stops being `value(t)` and becomes `value(φ)`, where φ is phase around
the cycle, 0..1, and the reader drives φ.

**And here is the good news, which is larger than it looks: the chapters are already written
against φ.** `KITE.fill`, `WALKER.fill`, `SHAPE.fill` (gather) and `CURTAIN.fill` are all
0..1 band scalars. The schedule enters only as the *target* those pursue. Point the target at
`reachFill(y)` directly instead of at `clamp01(normOf(B.value(t)))` and the figure is
reader-led that afternoon, in five chapters, with a one-line change in each.

What is then missing is everything about phase: which way they are going, where in the cycle
they are, and how fast. None of it exists, and all of it has to be derived.

## 1.3 The phase estimator — the one genuinely new component

From a single 0..1 signal `x(t)` the engine needs a direction, a cycle boundary and a period.

**Do not differentiate.** `dx/dt` on a finger is noise, and the project has already learned
the general form of this mistake four times over (CLAUDE.md, "my own test code is the most
common source of false alarms"). A derivative-based direction will chatter at every turn and
at every moment the reader holds still, which is exactly the moment the box protocol most
needs to be read correctly.

**Use a hysteretic turning-point detector.** Track the running extremum since the last turn;
declare a turn when `x` has retraced from it by more than a deadband `H`. The existing
`DEAD = 0.012` is far too small for this — it is a pursuit deadband, not a turn deadband.
A turn wants something in the region of 8–12% of the band, which is also roughly the finger
jitter of a hand resting on glass.

Three states fall out for free, and they are the three the Shepherd's harness already proved a
chapter needs: **rising**, **falling**, and **neither** — which is a hold, detected rather
than scheduled. That is the first thing in this design that is strictly better than what
exists: today a hold is a phase the app declares and the reader sits through; here a hold is
something the reader *does* and the scene recognises.

**The period is the time between successive same-direction turns.** Smooth it geometrically —
a one-pole on `log f`, not on `f` — because rates are ratios and a reader going from 12/min to
6/min should read as one step, not as six. The same reasoning is already in §3.3's cascade
table, where the steps get gentler as the rate falls.

**It must have an answer before the first cycle completes.** This is the hard part, and it is
where §1.7's opening clause does real work: *"starting with stillness and having the person
initiate the sequence rather than be subject to it."* If the scene is still until they move,
the first motion is itself the first datum, and the period can be seeded from the *speed* of
the first excursion — a reader who crosses the band in 0.4 s is not breathing at 4 s. Seed
from excursion speed; refine from turning points; never show a number.

## 1.4 Superstructures — what is superimposed, on what, and by what rule

The author's definition names its own failure mode, and that is the most useful sentence in
it: *"in order to normalise or stabilise them closer to a particular rhythm **without causing
them to vanish**."*

That rules out the two obvious implementations.

- **It is not a low-pass filter.** A filter on a panicked input makes the panic vanish. The
  output is smooth and the reader's rhythm has been deleted, which is exactly the
  "disregarding their motions as irrelevant" the principle forbids.
- **It is not a quantiser.** Snapping their turns onto a grid makes them vanish into the grid.

What stabilises without erasing is **coupling between two oscillators, not filtering of one
signal.**

> The reader's input is one oscillator: phase φ_r, frequency f_r, measured.
> The scene carries a second: phase φ_s, frequency f_s — **that is the superstructure.**
> They are coupled by a phase-attraction term:
>
>     dφ_s/dt = f_s + K · sin(φ_r − φ_s)

This is the standard Adler/Kuramoto coupling, and every property §1.7 asks for is a known
property of it:

- **Below a critical coupling the two run free.** The reader's rhythm is never overwritten,
  because capture is conditional and not automatic.
- **Above it they lock, and the locked frequency sits *between* the two.** The reader is
  pulled toward the app's rate without being forced onto it. That is "find synchronicity where
  their motion can align with our general patterns" with no metaphor left in it.
- **Lock is gradual and detuning-dependent** — an Arnold tongue. A reader far off the app's
  rate is not captured at all, which is right: nothing should fight a panicking person. A
  reader close to it falls in, which is right too: that is an invitation being accepted.
- **Their irregularity survives as modulation**, because φ_r is still driving. The cycle-to-
  cycle variation is visible in the scene rather than smoothed out of it. That is the
  "without causing them to vanish" clause, satisfied by the mechanism rather than by a tuning
  constant.

**And the "fraction off the 2s" is one number.** Set the superstructure's own frequency
slightly *below* the reader's measured rate — a fixed proportion, not a fixed offset — and the
lock point drags them down as they follow. §3.3 has already derived the right size of that
proportion for a different reason: **6–11% per step, nothing above about 12%.** Under §1.7 the
same figure becomes the detuning, and the cascade stops being a list of rungs and becomes a
continuous descent that the reader is walking themselves.

One consequence worth stating because it affects the music layer: holding BPM fixed while the
reader's rate drifts means the breath spans a **non-integer** number of beats. That is fine,
and §3.3's whole-beat rule was only ever load-bearing for the *breath envelope*. Nothing else
in the loop is locked to the breath (§3.4 rule 2: only the breath layer is strictly periodic),
so the music does not have to know.

## 1.5 How a motif goes continuously from 4-4-4-4 to 0.5-0.5

Two things have to change, and they are separate problems.

### (a) The ratio — stop representing a cycle as a list of phases

A 4-4-4-4 cycle has four phases; 0.5-0.5 has two. You cannot scale durations continuously from
one to the other, because the holds have to reach zero and a phase reaching zero is a
discontinuity in the phase *count*. Every chapter would need a branch at the moment it
happened, and the whole value of `breath.js` is that two protocols drive one scene with no
branches anywhere.

So represent the cycle as **a closed curve in (phase, value)**: the breath as a loop.
4-4-4-4 is a loop flat at the top and bottom for a quarter each; 0.5-0.5 is a loop with no
flats. **The flat fraction is one scalar, `h ∈ [0, ¼]`**, and `h → 0` continuously as the rate
rises. No phase ever vanishes; the flat just gets shorter.

Three things this buys:

- It is physiologically honest. The holds are the first thing a fast breath loses anyway.
- **§2.4's "holds stay short and exactly equal to the other phases" is satisfied by the shape
  of the parameterisation rather than by a check**, because `h` is *defined* as equal-to-the-
  others at its maximum and shorter everywhere else. That is the discipline §1.6a already
  taught: satisfy a rule with the shape of the mapping, never with a number tuned until it
  just clears the line.
- The limit is right. As `h → 0` the turn must not become a corner, and a loop with no flats
  and C1 turns is a sinusoid — which is what a fast breath is.

### (b) The imagery — "periphery fuzzy and less detailed at higher speeds, centre locked"

Two of the three pieces already exist.

**What exists.** Long Air carries a reader-driven, C2-smoothed scalar straight into the
fragment stage: `CLOUDIN = tracker(0, 2.6)` fed by how deep the glider has flown into a cloud,
arriving as `uInCloud`, and the cloud shader does `alpha *= 1.0 - uInCloud * 0.92` — up to 92%
of a whole surface class removed by a reader-driven number. `js/mesh.js` also carries
`nearKeep`/`nearGone`, a hard-terrace per-cell dissolve, currently driven by camera distance.
And `DRAG.speedFactor` already modulates the streaks.

**What is missing.** A rate to drive any of it with, and a *radial* parameterisation — today
the dissolve is driven by distance from the camera, not by distance from frame centre. That is
one extra uniform and one extra term.

**And one trap, which the project's own history predicts.** *Periphery fuzzy* must **not** be
a dissolve. Long Air already tried a stochastic per-cell discard on an opaque surface at 1.4 m
and 0.17 m cells; it read as torn card and as speckled static respectively, and the conclusion
recorded was that no cell size resolves it because it is a dither.

> **Implement "less detailed" as band-count reduction and octave reduction toward the frame
> edge, not as a dissolve.** Drop `posteriseAA`'s `n` and drop the fine material octaves as
> screen radius rises. The centre keeps its full band count and all its octaves.

That is literally less detail; it is *more* §4.2 at the edge, not less; and it is free —
fewer `smoothstep`s and fewer `sin`s. Which gives the one genuinely pleasing structural result
in this document:

> **The panic LOD and the performance budget are the same lever. Going faster costs less to
> draw.** At the moment the reader most needs the frame rate, the frame gets cheaper.

## 1.6 Where the invitation lives, with no instructional text anywhere

Today the schedule *is* the target. Under §1.7 the schedule becomes **a second mark in the
same band** — the place the figure would be if the reader were breathing a fraction slower.
Aiming at it is voluntary, costs nothing, and gains nothing, which is §1.6b's exact test.

But there is a stronger version, and it is the one I would argue for, because it closes a
different open wound in this project.

The Murmuration's post-mortem established that **a frame-fraction figure means nothing unless
something else in the frame is holding still**, and Lantern Current was withdrawn because its
breath moved something relative to nothing. A reader-led scene has *exactly* that hazard in a
sharper form: if the reader drives everything, there is no longer anything in frame that is
not them, and synchronicity becomes unreadable — you cannot see that you are in step with
something that is only ever yourself.

> **The superstructure is the fixed reference the book already discovered it needs.** It is
> the thing in frame that keeps the app's rhythm whether or not the reader follows it: a swell
> under the glider, the flock's own lazy boil, the far curtain of the rain, the sheep's pace.
> The reader's motion is read *against* it. Entrainment and legibility have one answer.

That also makes the invitation entirely imagery: nothing instructs, because the thing keeping
the slower rhythm is simply a part of the world, and the reader either falls in with it or
does not.

## 1.7 What breaks, named

These are the costs, in the order I would worry about them. Design doc §1.7a records the same
list in summary; this is the detail.

**(a) `regress.mjs`'s headline guarantee has no meaning under §1.7.** "On-pace play never
touches a surface" is a statement about a reader conforming to our rate, and there is no such
reader. The replacement is not a bigger sample; it is a **different kind of proof**: for every
reader trajectory in an admissible class (bounded speed, bounded reversal rate), the figure
never touches a surface. That is reachability, and in this engine it reduces to an inequality
between the corridor's half-width and the maximum excursion the saturated pursuit controller
can produce given the rate cap — `PURSUE_K`, the cap, `GATE_HALF`, `FORGIVE_MAX`. **That is
stronger than the current check and cheaper to run**, because it is arithmetic on constants
rather than a sampled flight. It is also a rewrite of the harness's whole structure and should
not be underestimated for being shorter.

**(b) §1.6a becomes a continuous property.** Today "no inhale underwater" is checked by
sampling phases at known times. Under §1.7 the reader can begin an inhale at any depth, so
sampling cannot establish it. The only safe construction is geometric: **the band's
fill-increasing region must lie entirely above the waterline**, checked once on the corridor,
statically. Again stronger than what exists — it is an invariant rather than a sample — and
again it is the §1.6a lesson restated: satisfy the rule with the shape of the mapping.

**(c) `breath.js` stops being `f(t)`.** `at`, `value`, `cue`, `travel` and `progress` all lose
the property that the same `t` gives the same answer. `cue()` and the whole `count` machinery
become dead, which costs nothing since there is no instructional text left.

**(d) The session clock and the cycle clock split, and that split is the clean part.** §1.1 of
the design document says an acute state shift takes minutes, so the *session* should still end
on elapsed time. Only the *cycle* becomes the reader's.

> **Time still owns the session. The reader owns the breath.**

**(e) Two chapters do not fit the model as built.** Pearl Diver's control is a one-shot latch
with no position and no duration; Bamboo Rain's is a binary press. Neither can express a
rate at all, so neither can be made reader-led without a new control. Pearl Diver's is the
harder of the two and is entangled with §1.6a; Bamboo Rain's is the easier, because its
scalar is already a 0..1 curtain position and only the input is binary.

## 1.8 Order I would build it in, if asked

Stated so the author can price it, not as a plan I am starting.

1. The phase estimator, standalone and headless, with a harness that feeds it recorded and
   synthetic finger traces — including a panicking one — and asserts the period it reports.
   Nothing in the app changes. This is the piece that can be wrong quietly.
2. The loop parameterisation (`h`), proved to reproduce all five existing protocols exactly at
   their own `h`, so the change is known to be a generalisation and not a replacement.
3. One chapter, reader-led, with the superstructure as a visible second rhythm. The
   Murmuration is the right one: it is the cheapest to draw by two orders of magnitude, it has
   no terrain to invalidate, and it is the chapter currently diagnosed as not reading — so
   there is the least to lose and an existing problem to solve.
4. The reachability proof, replacing the on-pace assertion, before any chapter with a surface
   in it (Long Air, the Shepherd) is converted.

# 2 · Can the graphics go up another order of magnitude?

## 2.1 The short answer

**Within the idiom: about half an order is available, and it is not where anyone has been
looking for it. An actual order of magnitude requires changing the idiom, and the thing it
would cost is the one rule the project most relies on. I would not change it.**

## 2.2 What the idiom actually is, in code

- **No lighting model at all.** No `DirectionalLight`, no `MeshStandardMaterial`, no shadow
  map, no tone map, no post pipeline, no `envMap`, and — worth saying out loud — **no texture
  sampling anywhere in the project.** Every surface is procedural in the fragment stage.
- **`N·L` exists in exactly three places**, each a two- or three-value terminator on a small
  round object: the Shepherd's fleece, Pearl Diver's pearl/dove, and (not really `N·L` at all)
  the Murmuration's birds, which use a heading dot product. Everything else — the ridge, the
  hill, the seabed, the water, the kelp, the gates, the trees — is **flat value as a function
  of a hand-chosen field coordinate**, banded.
- **Normals exist on three things.** `builder()` never computes one. The ridge, the hill, the
  water plane and the rest genuinely do not have them.
- **The palette is six values, by contract.** `ramp()` throws if given anything other than six
  steps. The binding sentence is `palette.js:15-23`: *the indices are assigned by hand, per
  layer, never derived from distance or from a lighting calculation* — because when Deep Light
  derived them from depth, "the frame goes flat; every object reads as the same object at a
  different distance."
- **The project's band count is 3.** Nearly every surface in the book posterises at 3; a few
  at 4, 5 or 6.

## 2.3 Where the ceiling actually is

It is **a palette ceiling, not a resolution ceiling.** Six hand-assigned values per surface is
the constraint, and it is the same line that makes two objects at the same distance read as
different things. You cannot get an order of magnitude more *information* out of six values.

What you can get more of is **fields** — more independent scalars being banded and combined.
Long Air's ridge already runs about ten of them (profile tone, crest band, grass comb,
wildflower clumps, spine split, two material octaves, grain, low air, fog) over a palette of
six ramp steps plus five named colours. That composition is where its apparent detail comes
from, and it scales. It costs fragment time, which — see §3 — is the only budget that is
actually binding.

> **More detail inside this idiom means more fields. It does not mean more bands and it
> certainly does not mean more triangles.**

Three things already recorded that say the same from other directions:

- *"Geometry buys the silhouette and the way light falls across a slope; it does not buy
  grain."* The two things the reader named as reading like materials — the sail's weave, the
  cloud's wool — are both shading.
- The grass comb was first written as a ±0.45 shift of the ramp *index*, which stayed inside
  one cell and came out the same colour: **a correctly-evaluated detail layer eaten whole by
  the quantiser.** A six-step lookup discards most of what you feed it.
- A terminator on a ten-ring sphere is a polygon however well the step is resolved. Resolution
  is not the lever either.

## 2.4 The one place the idiom demonstrably broke — and what the fix was

The Murmuration, in its own shader: *"A sky behind nothing is the case that breaks: with no
horizon to compress the gradient into the bottom of the picture, six steps across 812 px are
six SLABS, the edges read as horizons, and they cut straight through the flock... The idiom is
about edges that mean something; these mean nothing, so they go."*

**The fix was not continuous shading. It was more steps and dither** — three overlapping
`posteriseAA` calls at 8, 8 and 6 (the "sixteen" in the note is the effective count of two
superimposed 8-step fields, not a literal 16 anywhere), the warm wash from 3 to 8, and a
per-pixel grain term on top.

That is the precedent, and it points at a specific, limited change rather than at an identity
decision:

> **Band count should be a per-surface parameter chosen by the subject, not a project
> constant.** Raise it where the subject has no meaningful edges — skies, open water, fog,
> large soft masses. Leave it at 3 where the subject does — figures, terrain silhouettes,
> gates, kelp, coral.

That is a rewrite of how §4.2 rule 5 is *applied*. It is not a rewrite of rule 1, and it does
not need §4.2 reopened.

There is a neat technical fact underneath it: `posteriseAA` already ends with
`mix(q, s/(n-1), clamp(w - 0.5, 0, 1))` — **where one pixel spans more than half a band, it
falls back to the continuous value and the bands dissolve entirely.** The two looks are
endpoints of one function the engine already has. Continuous shading is `n → ∞` on a limit
that is already built in.

## 2.5 What an actual order of magnitude would cost

It needs three things the engine lacks, none of them hard and all of them new:

1. **Normals on every surface.** On the ridge, which is `scrollGlsl`-displaced per vertex and
   uses `aXp`/`aZb` as surface coordinates, that is a change to the geometry pipeline, not a
   `computeVertexNormals()` call.
2. **A world light vector.** There is one derived elevation (22.6°, from the shadow being 2.4×
   the glider's height) and an **acknowledged-inconsistent azimuth** — the shadow falls
   forward, putting the light behind the reader, while the sky wash is painted ahead of them.
   That contradiction was resolved by deleting the sun disc, which works only as long as
   nothing in frame asserts a direction. A lighting model asserts one everywhere.
3. **A shadow mechanism.** Today: three hand-placed flat discs, present precisely because
   nothing is lit so nothing casts anything.

**What would be lost is `palette.js:15-23`** — hand-assigned value — and that is not a
technical cost, it is the identity cost §4.2a already flagged as not its decision to make. The
recorded result of deriving value instead of assigning it is on file: the frame goes flat.

## 2.6 Recommendation

**Keep the idiom. Spend the available half-order on three things, in this order:**

1. **Finish the §4.2a pass.** Bamboo Rain imports the *plain* prelude and has **zero**
   `aastep` and **fifteen** unresolved GLSL `step()`s, several on genuine per-fragment fields:
   two disc radii, a lightning-lit band, and the rain front itself. The Deep Light idle
   background — which is the cover's living backdrop, so it is the first thing anyone sees —
   has none of the AA layer either. **Two of six surfaces in the book are shipping the cheap
   version**, so any judgement about "the ceiling of the current idiom" formed by looking at
   the shipped book is partly a judgement about the unconverted half of it. This is the
   cheapest real improvement available and it needs no decision from anyone.
2. **Per-surface band counts**, as §2.4.
3. **More fields on the surfaces that carry the frame**, as §2.3 — which is where the fragment
   budget in §3 says there is room.

# 3 · Efficiency: where the cost is, and what the headroom is

## 3.1 Measured, on the deployed build, 12 November 2026

Netlify, 375×812 CSS, drawing buffer 562×1218, MSAA **4 samples / SAMPLE_BUFFERS 1** granted,
`DPR_CAP 1.5`. GPU: **AMD Radeon 780M via ANGLE/D3D11** — a laptop integrated part, which is
the caveat that governs this whole section.

| chapter | triangles | draw calls | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Shepherd** | **145,470** | **5** | CLAUDE.md records 81,920. **The note is stale.** |
| **Long Air** | **140,454** | 14 | CLAUDE.md records 140,526. Agrees. |
| **Pearl Diver** | **46,912** | **17** | never recorded anywhere |
| **Bamboo Rain** | **3,388** | 13 | never recorded anywhere |
| **The Murmuration** | **1,122** | 2 | agrees |

Two of the five chapters had no cost figure on record at all, and the one for the chapter that
has changed most recently is wrong by 78%. Worth fixing in CLAUDE.md.

## 3.2 The bench is not trustworthy, and here is the number

Eight consecutive `bench(60)` runs on one page, same scene, same buffer, inside sixty seconds:

```
0.100  0.085  0.100  0.057  0.062  0.098  0.192  0.077   ms/render
min 0.057   max 0.192   ratio 3.4×
```

**3.4× spread on identical work.** CLAUDE.md's warning that the sandbox bench "has swung 30×
on identical builds" is not an exaggeration, and any single reading from it means nothing.
Everything below uses medians of eight.

I also attempted a resolution-scaling test — double the drawing buffer, see whether frame time
follows — and **it could not be run**: changing the emulated viewport did not propagate to the
drawing buffer, which stayed at 768×1024 through a 2× viewport change. Reported rather than
worked around, because a scaling test whose independent variable did not move is not a
measurement.

## 3.3 The finding that matters: the book is fragment-bound, and the triangle budget is not
the binding constraint

Medians of eight, at comparable buffer sizes:

| | triangles | buffer | median ms/render | ms per megapixel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long Air | 140,454 | 768×1024 = 786k px | **0.083** | 0.106 |
| Bamboo Rain | 3,388 | 562×1218 = 685k px | **~0.090** | 0.131 |

> **Forty-one times the geometry, the same frame time.** Cost tracks pixels, not primitives.

This is worth sitting with, because it contradicts the anxiety that has been driving several
passes. CLAUDE.md has been tracking the triangle count nervously since 3 November — "that is
where I would start being nervous", "the next structural change will want another 20%" — and
on this GPU the triangle count is close to irrelevant. The Shepherd carries *more* geometry
than Long Air in **five** draw calls and costs less.

What that implies, concretely:

- **Subdivision is cheap and the decision to buy columns was right**, for a reason nobody
  stated: it was never the expensive axis.
- **The expensive axis is per-fragment arithmetic on the surfaces that fill the frame.** The
  heaviest fragment `main()` in the book is Long Air's ridge — roughly **12 `sin`, 31
  `smoothstep`, 13 `fwidth`, 1 `sqrt`, 1 `exp`, ~16 vec3 `mix`** per fragment, over the lower
  two thirds of the frame. Pearl Diver's water plane is heavier in transcendentals
  (**~18 `sin`, 24 `smoothstep`, 14 `fwidth`**) and, structurally, **computes both the
  above-water and below-water paths unconditionally every fragment and blends them by
  `uAbove`.**
- **The single largest identifiable waste is the ramp lookup.** `rampPickAA` is a 5-iteration
  unrolled loop of `smoothstep` + `fwidth`. The ridge calls it three times — **15 smoothsteps
  and 3 derivative queries, about half the shader's entire smoothstep budget, to choose
  between six colours.** Only one of the five boundaries is ever near the fragment's value.
  Computing the band index once and resolving only the adjacent riser is a straightforward
  3–4× cut on the dominant cost of the dominant surface.
- **Pearl Diver's 17 draw calls** are the outlier against the Shepherd's 5. The Shepherd is the
  model and the technique is already written up — one merged mesh with per-animal uniform
  arrays.

## 3.4 The actual headroom on a phone — honestly, it is unknown

I cannot measure it and neither has anyone else. What can be said:

- On this desktop iGPU the heaviest chapter draws in ~0.083 ms against a 16.7 ms budget. Even
  granting the 3.4× instrument spread *and* a phone being an order of magnitude slower, the
  instantaneous margin is large.
- **The instantaneous margin is not the constraint. Thermal is.** A session is minutes long, a
  phone in a hand throttles, and a chapter that renders at 60 fps for thirty seconds and 40 fps
  at four minutes is a chapter that fails exactly when the breathing is working. Nothing in
  this project has ever measured that, and no amount of sandbox benching will.
- `DPR_CAP = 1.5` is correctly identified as the single biggest lever and is already engaged.
  Because the book is fragment-bound, it is also the *only* lever with an order of magnitude
  in it.

**The cheap way to get the number, which I would do before anything else in §2.6:** every
chapter already exposes `__chapter.bench()`. Add a median-frame-time readout behind an Options
toggle — no new instrument, just surfacing one that exists — and have the reader read it off
their own phone at the start and at the end of a five-minute session. Two numbers, from the
only device that matters. Everything else in this section is inference from the wrong
hardware.

# 4 · What shipped on 12 November

Not analysis. Pushed, deployed and looked at, at 375 px.

**The cover.** The board was `#4A2418` — a mid chocolate brown with no red in it, against a
brief that has always said oxblood; it is `#4C1420` now. The grain was three
repeating-linear-gradients at 26°, −54° and 0° on a 1 px pitch, which is a woven plaid whose
diagonals beat against each other and whose 1 px stripes land sub-pixel on a 3× phone; leather
grain has no direction, and it is two `feTurbulence` tiles now, fine over coarse. The gold had
no direction at all — every rule `2px solid` on all four sides, on a face that has carried a
highlight at 18% 6% since it was written — and now has per-side bevel colours and a groove.
The stitch was one dashed hairline, which is a dotted line; thread sits *on* leather and has a
shadow, and the shadow copy is `inset:0` rather than `-1px` because a dashed border's phase
depends on the side length.

The corner ornaments are struck rather than flat, and **the four corners no longer mirror their
own light**: a deboss written as `translate(0, +0.9)` becomes `−0.9` under `scaleY(-1)`, so the
bottom pair were lit from below and the right pair ran uphill. The flip is passed in and the
light-bearing quantities are pre-compensated in SVG space.

The title is gold leaf — a `background-clip` gradient with a bright top, a dark belly and a lit
lower lip — rather than a pale yellow word with a drop shadow. The "Open" affordance was a
999 px pill, the one element on the cover that belonged to a settings sheet; it is a tooled
cartouche now, same padding, same word, same tap target.

**One thing was built, deployed, looked at and replaced**, which is the §4.2a discipline
arriving in CSS: the medallion rim was a conic gradient. A conic has a seam at its start angle,
visible as a notch on a 106 px rim, and clipping it to a ring needs a radial mask with hard
stops — an unresolved edge on a curve, where MSAA cannot reach it either. A border on a circle
gives the whole thing for nothing: antialiased by the browser, blended across the diagonals, no
start angle and therefore no seam.

**The scroll.** The paper was graph paper — 1 px every 4 and every 7, *both* axes, which is a
grid and the one thing laid paper never is. Laid paper is fine laid lines one way and sparse
chain lines the other; it is that now, with a turbulence mottle so the lines are not perfectly
even. The sheet came from nowhere — a rectangle with a bar above and below and a visible gap at
each — and now goes under the rod. The rods were pills; turned wood has a narrow specular line
and a finial is a disc seen edge-on. Headings went from bold sans to serif. **Small caps were
tried and rejected**: these headings are whole sentences and one of them is the water rule, and
a sentence set in small caps is read more slowly. The anachronism being fixed was the sans, and
it is not better fixed by making the line that matters harder to read.

**And one bug found on the way.** `sw.js` tested only `js|html` for network-first, so
`css/bookframe.css` — the single definition of the gold tooling, the board and the fore-edge —
was served **cache-first**. This pass would have been invisible on a returning reader's device
until some unrelated cache bump. That is the failure already written up under "deployment can
hide a fix", one file type over. CSS is code; the test says so now.
